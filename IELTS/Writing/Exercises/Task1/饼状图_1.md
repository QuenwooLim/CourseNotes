### Grammar & Spelling Check

#### Case 1

Original: how energy **using** in an average Australian household

Corrected: how energy **is used** in an average Australian household

Reason: Passive voice is required here. “Energy” cannot perform the action itself.

#### Case 2

Original: the water heating **account**

Corrected: water heating **accounts**

Reason: Subject-verb agreement error. “Water heating” is singular.

#### Case 3

Original: In **Australian**

Corrected: In **Australia**

Reason: “Australian” is an adjective, while “Australia” is the correct noun.

#### Case 4

Original: water heating **keeps** a similar proportion

Corrected: water heating **maintains** a similar proportion

Reason: “Maintains” is more natural and academic in Task 1 writing.

#### Case 5

Original: account for 15%, 7%, 4% and 2% for household energy use **individually**

Corrected: account for 15%, 7%, 4% and 2% of household energy use **respectively**

Reason: “Respectively” is the correct word when matching percentages to categories.

#### Case 6

Original: in Australian

Corrected: in Australia

Reason: Incorrect word form.

#### Case 7

Original: with the percentage of 28%, 14%, 8%, 3%

Corrected: with percentages of 28%, 14%, 8%, and 3%

Reason: More grammatically accurate and natural expression.

### Corrected Essay

The two pie charts compare how energy is used in an average Australian household and the greenhouse gas emissions produced by this energy use.

It is clear that heating accounts for the largest proportion of household energy use in Australia, while water heating produces the highest percentage of greenhouse gas emissions. In contrast, cooling and lighting contribute only a small proportion in both categories.

In Australia, 42% of household energy is used for heating, whereas this activity is responsible for only 15% of greenhouse gas emissions. Meanwhile, water heating represents a similar proportion in both household energy use and greenhouse gas emissions, at around 30% and 32% respectively.

Other appliances, refrigeration, lighting, and cooling account for 15%, 7%, 4%, and 2% of household energy use respectively. However, these categories produce higher proportions of greenhouse gas emissions in Australia, at 28%, 14%, 8%, and 3% respectively.
