# Sentence-by-Sentence Review

## Case 1

### Original

The line graph compares the goods transported between four different modes of transport in UK from 1974 to 2002.

### Corrected

The line graph compares the quantity of goods transported by four different modes of transport in **the** UK from 1974 to 2002.

### Reason

“By” is more appropriate than “between,” and “the UK” requires an article. “Quantity” is also more natural in academic writing.

---

## Case 2

### Original

It's clear that road was the most important way for goods transporting, while the quantities of goods that pipeline transported was the lowest.

### Corrected

It is clear that road transport was the most important method of **transporting goods**, while the quantity of goods transported by pipeline was the lowest.

### Reason

More formal academic language is needed in IELTS Task 1. The sentence structure was also simplified for clarity.

---

## Case 3

### Original

Besides rail, the other three modes of transport kept a upward trend over a period of 28 years.

### Corrected

Apart from rail, the other three modes of transport showed **an** upward trend over the 28-year period.

### Reason

“Showed an upward trend” is more natural and academic than “kept an upward trend”.

# Corrected Essay

The line graph compares the quantity of goods transported by four different modes of transport in the UK from 1974 to 2002.

It is clear that road transport was the dominant mode of transporting goods throughout the period, while pipeline transport accounted for the smallest quantity. Apart from rail, the other three modes generally showed an upward trend over the 28 years.

In 1974, the quantity of goods transported by road was about 70 million tonnes, which was by far the highest figure. Water and rail each transported around 40 million tonnes of goods, while pipeline transport accounted for only a negligible amount. Four years later, water transport caught up with rail and then increased by about 20 million tonnes over the following four years.

The quantity of goods transported by rail remained at nearly 40 million tonnes, which was similar to the figure in 1974. As for water and pipeline transport, both figures rose by about 20 million tonnes over the 28-year period, and the most significant increase took place between 1978 and 1982.
