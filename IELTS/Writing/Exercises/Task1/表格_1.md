### Grammar & Spelling Check

#### Case 1

Original: UK **saled** the most Fairtrade-labelled coffee in 2004

Corrected: UK **sold** the most Fairtrade-labelled coffee in 2004

Reason: Incorrect past tense form of the verb “sell”.

#### Case 2

Original: 1.5 **millions** of euros

Corrected: 1.5 **million** euros

Reason: “Million” should not be pluralized when used with a specific number.

#### Case 3

Original: the sales of Switzerland were 3 **millions** of euros

Corrected: the sales in Switzerland were 3 **million** euros

Reason: Incorrect plural form and awkward expression.

#### Case 4

Original: **althought**

Corrected: **although**

Reason: Spelling mistake.

#### Case 5

Original: presented **an rising** trend

Corrected: presented **a rising** trend

Reason: “Rising” begins with a consonant sound, so “a” should be used instead of “an”.

#### Case 6

Original: trend **,**

Corrected: trend**,**

Reason: Unnecessary space before punctuation.

#### Case 7

Original: ranked **the first**

Corrected: ranked **first**

Reason: Incorrect article usage.

#### Case 8

Original: with 20 **millions** of euros

Corrected: with 20 **million** euros

Reason: “Million” should remain singular after a number.

#### Case 9

Original: it was **no doubt** that

Corrected: it was **undoubtedly true** that

Reason: Incorrect expression. “There was no doubt that” is also acceptable.

#### Case 10

Original: **whild** Sweden and Denmark decreased

Corrected: **while** Sweden and Denmark decreased

Reason: Spelling mistake.

### Corrected Essay

The tables compare the sales of Fairtrade-labelled coffee and bananas in five European countries in 1999 and 2004.

The UK sold the most Fairtrade-labelled coffee in 2004, while Switzerland was the largest seller of bananas in both 1999 and 2004. The sales of these Fairtrade-labelled goods increased significantly over the five-year period in most countries, except for Sweden and Denmark in terms of bananas.

In 1999, 1.5 million euros’ worth of coffee was sold in the UK, while the figure for Switzerland was 3 million euros. However, although Switzerland showed a rising trend, the UK overtook Switzerland in 2004 and ranked first with 20 million euros in sales. The other three countries also experienced a slight upward trend from 1999 to 2004.

As for bananas, there was no doubt that Switzerland accounted for the largest proportion of sales, while the other countries made up a relatively small share. Over the five-year period, Switzerland, the UK, and Belgium all experienced substantial increases, while Sweden and Denmark saw declines.
