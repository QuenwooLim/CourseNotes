Inside the mind of a fan

粉丝的内心世界

How watching sport affects the brain

观看体育比赛如何影响大脑

A  At about the same time that the poet Homer invented the epic hero, the ancient Greeks started a festival in which men competed in a single race, about 200 metres long. The winner received a branch of wild olives. The Greeks called this celebration the Olympics. Through the ancient sprint remains, today the Olympics are far more than that. Indeed, the Games seem to celebrate the dream of progress as embodied in the human form. That the Games are intoxicating to watch is beyond question. During the Athens Olympics in 2004, 3.4 billion people, half the world watched them on television. Certainly, being a spectator is a thrilling experience but why?

A  大约在诗人荷马创造出史诗英雄的同一时期，古希腊人开始举办一个节日，男人们在其中进行一项约200米长的赛跑。获胜者会得到一支野生橄榄枝。希腊人将这个庆祝活动称为奥运会。通过古老的短跑项目（流传至今），如今的奥运会已远不止于此。的确，奥运会似乎是在庆祝以人类形体所体现的进步梦想。奥运会令人陶醉是毋庸置疑的。在2004年雅典奥运会期间，全球34亿人，即一半的世界人口，通过电视观看了比赛。当然，做一名观众是一种激动人心的体验，但这是为什么呢？

B  In 1996, three Italian neuroscientists, Giacomo Rizzolatti, Leonardo Fogassi and Vittorio Gallese, examined the premotor cortex of monkeys. 「Q33」 They discovered that inside these primate brains there were groups of cells that ‘store vocabularies of motor actions’. Just as there are grammars of movement. These networks of cells are the bodily ‘sentences’ we use every day, the ones our brain has chosen to retain and refine. Think, for example, about a golf swing. To those who have only watched the Masters’ Tournament on TV, golfing seems easy. To the novice, however, the skill of casting a smooth are with a lop-side metal stick is vitally impossible. 「Q28」 「Q36」 This is because most novices swing with their consciousness, using an area of brain next to the premotor cortex. To the expert, on the other hand, a perfectly balanced stroke is second nature. 「Q34」 For him, the motor action has become memorized, and the movements are embedded in the neurons of his premotor cortex. He hits the ball with the tranquility of his perfected autopilot.
B  1996年，三位意大利神经科学家——贾科莫·里佐拉蒂、莱昂纳多·福加西和维托里奥·加莱塞——检查了猴子的前运动皮层。他们发现，在这些灵长类动物的大脑中，存在一些细胞群，它们“储存着运动动作的词汇库”。就像存在动作的语法一样。这些细胞网络是我们每天使用的身体“句子”，是我们大脑选择保留和精炼的。以高尔夫挥杆为例。对于只在电视上看过大师锦标赛的人来说，打高尔夫似乎很容易。然而，对于新手来说，用一根笨重的金属棒划出平滑弧线的技能几乎是不可能的。这是因为大多数新手是用意识来挥杆，使用的是前运动皮层旁边的一个脑区。而另一方面，对于专家来说，一个完美平衡的击球是第二天性。对他而言，运动动作已经成为记忆，这些动作被嵌入了他前运动皮层的神经元中。他是以完美的自动导航状态的平静来击球的。

C  These neurons in the premotor cortex, besides explaining why certain athletes seem to possess almost unbelievable levels of skill, have an even more amazing characteristic, one that caused Rizzolatti, Fogassi and Gallese to give them the lofty title ‘mirror neurons’. They note, ‘The main functional characteristic of mirror neurons is that they become active both when the monkey performs a particular action (for example, grasping an object or holding it and, astonishingly, when it sees another individual performing a similar action.’ 「Q37」 「Q30」 Humans have an even more elaborate mirror neuron system. These peculiar cells mirror, inside the brain, the outside world: they enable us to internalise the actions of another. In order to be activated, though, these cells require what the scientists call ‘goal-orientated movements’. 「Q38」 If we are staring at a photograph, a fixed image of a runner mid-stride, our mirror neurons are totally silent. They only fire when the runner is active: running, moving of sprinting.

C  前运动皮层中的这些神经元，除了能解释为什么某些运动员似乎拥有几乎不可思议的技能水平外，还有一个更惊人的特性，这个特性让里佐拉蒂、福加西和加莱塞赋予它们一个崇高的称号——“镜像神经元”。他们指出，“镜像神经元的主要功能特征是，当猴子执行某个特定动作（例如，抓取物体或握住它）时，这些神经元会变得活跃，而且令人惊讶的是，当它看到另一个个体执行类似动作时，它们也会变得活跃。”人类拥有更为精密的镜像神经元系统。这些奇特的细胞在大脑内部镜像外部世界：它们使我们能够将他人的动作内化。不过，为了被激活，这些细胞需要科学家所说的“目标导向的运动”。如果我们盯着照片看，比如一张跑步者中途迈步的静态图像，我们的镜像神经元是完全沉默的。只有当跑步者处于活动状态时——奔跑、移动或冲刺——它们才会激发。

D  What these electrophysiological studies indicate is that when we watch a golfer or a runner in action, the mirror neurons in our own premotor cortex light up as if we were the ones competing 「Q31」 . This phenomenon was first discovered in 1954, when two French physiologists, Gastaut and Bert, identified a distinct brain wave called the mu rhythm. The mu rhythm is involved in neural mirroring 「Q40」 . It is active when your bodies are still, and disappears whenever we do something active, like playing sport or changing the TV channel. The surprising fact is that the mu signal is also quiet when we watch someone else being active, as on TV, these results are the effect of mirror neurons.

D  这些电生理学研究表明，当我们观看高尔夫球手或跑步者的动作时，我们自己前运动皮层中的镜像神经元会活跃起来，就好像是我们自己在比赛一样。这一现象最早于1954年被发现，当时两位法国生理学家加斯托（Gastaut）和贝尔（Bert）识别出了一种独特的脑电波，称为μ波。μ波参与神经镜像过程。当你的身体静止时，它处于活跃状态；而当我们做一些积极的动作时，比如运动或切换电视频道，它就会消失。令人惊讶的事实是，当我们观看别人（比如在电视上）活动时，μ信号也会变得安静，这些结果就是镜像神经元的作用。

E  Rizzolatti, Fogassi and Gallese call the idea of mirror neurons the‘direct matching hypothesis’. They believe that we only understand the movement of sports stars when we‘map the visual representation of the observed action onto our motor representation of the same action’. According to this theory, watching an Olympic athlete‘causes the motor system of the observer to resonate. The “motor knowledge” of the observer is used to understand the observed action.’ But mirror neurons are more than just the neural basis for our attitude to sport. 「Q32」 It turns out that watching a great golfer makes us better golfers, and watching a great sprinter actually makes us run faster. This ability to learn by watching is a crucial skill. From the acquisition of language as infants to learning facial expressions, mimesis (copying) is an essential part of being conscious. 「Q29」 The best athletes are those with a premotor cortex capable of imagining the movements of victory, together with the physical properties to make those movements real.
E  里佐拉蒂、福加西和加莱塞将镜像神经元的观点称为“直接匹配假说”。他们认为，只有当我们“将观察到的动作的视觉表征映射到我们自己对该动作的运动表征上”时，我们才能理解体育明星的动作。根据这一理论，观看奥运选手“会导致观察者的运动系统产生共振。观察者的‘运动知识’被用来理解所观察到的动作。”但镜像神经元不仅仅是构成我们对待体育态度的神经基础。事实证明，观看伟大的高尔夫球手能让我们成为更好的高尔夫球手，观看伟大的短跑运动员实际上能让我们跑得更快。这种通过观察学习的能力是一项至关重要的技能。从婴儿时期学习语言到学习面部表情，模仿（复制）是意识的重要组成部分。最优秀的运动员是那些拥有能够想象胜利动作的前运动皮层，同时拥有使这些动作成为现实的身体素质的人。

F   「Q35」 But how many of us regularly watch sports in order to be a better athlete? Rather, we watch sport for the feeling, the human drama. 「Q27」 This feeling also derives from mirror neurons. By letting spectators share in the motions of victory, they also allow us to share in its feelings. 「Q27」 This is because they are directly connected to the amygdale, one of the main brain regions involved in emotion. During the Olympics, the mirror neurons of whole nations will be electrically identical, their athletes causing spectators to feel, just for a second or two, the same thing. Watching sports brings people together. Most of us will never run a mile in under four minutes, or hit a home run. Our consolation comes in watching, when we gather around the TV, we all feel, just for a moment, what it is to do something perfectly.

F  但是我们中有多少人为了成为更好的运动员而定期观看体育比赛呢？相反，我们看体育比赛是为了感觉，为了人间戏剧。这种感觉也源于镜像神经元。通过让观众分享胜利的动作，它们也让我们分享胜利的感觉。这是因为它们直接连接到杏仁核，这是大脑中涉及情绪的主要区域之一。在奥运会期间，整个国家的镜像神经元在电生理上是相同的，他们的运动员让观众在那一两秒钟内感受到同样的东西。观看体育比赛使人们走到一起。我们大多数人永远不会在四分钟内跑完一英里，也不会打出本垒打。我们的慰藉来自于观看，当我们围在电视机旁时，我们都会在那一刻感受到完美地做成一件事是什么样的感觉。
