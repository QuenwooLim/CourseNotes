The Art of Persuasion

说服的艺术

A Whether we are aware of it or not, all around us, “persuaders” are at work. We have become used to a world where powerful images, catchy soundbites and pushy advertising bombard us every day. Supermarkets, chain stores, politicians, advertisers and salespeople know exactly how to manipulate us with their images, ideas or produce. When it comes to persuading us，they employ skilled experts to use the best psychological tricks, meaning even the cautious among us may be influenced without realizing it.

不管我们是否意识到，在我们周围，“说服者”都在起作用。我们已经习惯了一个每天都有强大的图像、朗朗上口的声音和咄咄逼人的广告轰炸我们的世界。超市、连锁商店、政客、广告商和销售者——人们知道如何利用他们的形象、想法或产品来操纵我们。当到了说服我们时，他们会聘请有经验的专家来使用最好的心理技巧，这意味着即使是我们当中的谨慎者也可能在没有意识到的情况下受到影响。

B Supermarkets have always led the way in this type of persuasion. We now buy 75% of our food from supermarket chains, and most of us leave having purchased products we didn't intend to buy. 「Q28」 In one early stroke of genius, Sainsbury introduced shopping baskets to its stores in the 1950s. This meant that shoppers would now browse and pick up items that they formerly would have ignored. Soon after came shopping trolleys, and the novelty attracted more shoppers. Professor Merlin Stone, IBM Professor of Relationship Marketing at Bristol Business School, says aisles are deliberately laid out to maximize profits 「Q35」 . Stores are designed around our money-rich, time-poor lives, with high-turnover products commanding positions at the front, whereas low-turnover products such as clothes and electrical goods stocked at the back of the store.

超市在这类说服中一直处于领先地位。我们现在75%的食品都是从连锁超市购买的，而我们中的大多数人在离开时购买了我们本来不打算购买的产品。 「Q28」 很早的一次天才之举是早在20世纪50年代，塞恩斯伯里超市在其门店中引入了购物篮。这意味着购物者现在可以浏览和挑选他们以前忽略的商品。不久之后，购物推车出现了，这个新奇的事物吸引了更多的顾客。布里斯托尔商学院IBM关系营销教授梅林·斯通表示, 过道的设计是为了实现利润最大化。 「Q35」 商店的设计是围绕着我们有钱却没时间的生活，高周转率的产品在前面，而低周转率的产品如衣服和电器在商店的后面。
C Ralph Stone, an associate professor at the University of Chicago, believes that stores use “stalling tactics” to keep us in the store longer, because the more time we spend, the more we will buy. They make great efforts to make the store environment pleasant, even playing pleasant music to induce a relaxed state of mind. Some stores take advantage of the air from the in-store bakery to tempt shoppers with the aroma. In the USA, fake aromas are sometimes used because studies have shown that smell is the most evocative and subliminal of our senses and even prompts us to spend more 「Q29」 . In one experiment, a casino that perfumed half of its building saw profits rise significantly in the scent-filled areas.  Another success story from the supermarkets' perspective is the loyalty card. Shoppers may feel that they are being rewarded for the royal patronage, but actually, they are also sharing information about their shopping habits and possibly paying more for the products than they would elsewhere.

芝加哥大学副教授拉夫·斯通认为，商店使用“拖延战术”让我们在店里停留得更久，因为我们花的时间越多，买的东西就会越多。他们努力使商店环境愉快，甚至播放愉快的音乐来诱导一个放松的心态。一些商店利用店内面包房的香味来吸引顾客。在美国，有时会使用假香味，因为研究表明，气味是我们感官中最能唤起作用和最具潜意识的，甚至会促使我们消费更多。 「Q29」 在一项实验中，一家在一半的建筑区域都喷了香水的赌场，其在充满香味的区域利润大幅增长。从超市的角度来看，另一个成功的故事是顾客忠诚卡。购物者可能会觉得他们因为忠实的光顾而得到了回报，但实际上，他们也在分享有关自己购物习惯的信息，而且可能会比在其他地方花更多的钱购买商品。

D 「Q30」 「Q40」 It doesn't matter how wise we think we are to their tactics-the adverting industry still manages to influence us. They focus on what product to buy or on how they make us feel，and researchers at the University of Florida recently concluded that our emotional responses to adverts dominate o 「Q34」 ver cognition. Researcher Laurette Dube, in the Journal of Advertising Research, says that when attitudes are based on logical reasoning, advertisers use informative appeals. This works particularly well for products with little emotional appeal but high functionality, such as bleach. On the other hand, when attitudes are based on emotions，advertisements will aim to tap into our feelings.

「Q30」 不管我们认为自己对待他们的策略有多明智，广告业仍然设法影响着我们。它们关注的是要买什么产品，或者这些产品给我们带来的感受。佛罗里达大学的研究人员最近得出结论，我们对广告的情感反应主导着认知。 「Q34」 研究人员劳蕾特·杜布在《广告研究杂志》上说，当人们的态度建立在逻辑推理的基础上时，广告商就会使用信息吸引。这种方法特别适用于情感吸引力小但功能强大的产品，如漂白剂。另一方面，当态度是建立在情感的基础上时，广告的目的是要利用我们的感情。

E Advertisers play on a wide range of our emotions. Some advertisements aim to be aspirational in order to appeal to our self-esteem. 「Q36」 Others focus on our need to feel safe, such as advertisements for insurance. Others, such as advertisements for fashion, make us desire to feel like we belong to a group. 「Q33」 Celebrities are often used as a “shortcut” to appeal to these needs, because they are already seen as “successful” or “familiar and safe” people. One survey of 4000 advertising campaigns found that advertisements with celebrities were ten percent more effective than those without. Humour is also used with success in advertising，because it stimulates a rapid emotional res 「Q37」 「Q31」 ponse-an article in the International Journal of Advertising by Hwiman Chung stated that amusing advertisements are remembered for longer than serious ones.

电子广告商利用我们各种各样的情感。一些广告旨在有雄心壮志是为了迎合我们的自尊。 「Q36」 另一些则关注我们对安全感的需求，如保险广告。其他的，如时尚广告，使我们渴望感觉我们属于一个群体。 「Q33」 名人常常被用作满足这些需求的“捷径”，因为他们已经被视为“成功人士”或“熟悉的和安全的”人。一项对4000个广告活动的调查发现，有名人代言的广告比没有名人代言的广告效果好10%。幽默也被成功地运用在广告中，因为它能刺激快速的情感反应-钟慧敏在《国际广告杂志》上发表的 「Q37」 「Q31」 一篇文章指出，有趣的广告比严肃的广告更容易被长久记住。
F Perhaps we could all tap into our “inner salesperson” for a product that we were truly passionate about, but professional salespeople are in a league of their own—they can sell a wide variety of products, and be flexible as to targeting customers at the right time for them. They use basic psychological techniques—after all, selling works by effectively increasing a shoppers perception of how much they need a product or service. Shoppers usually have pre-conceived requirements by which they judge the suitability of a produc 「Q32」 t Sellers attempt to find out what these requirements are and then exlpain their products in terms that will appeal to individual shoppers.

也许我们都可以为一个我们真正热爱的产品探寻我们的“内在销售人员”，但是专业的销售人员是独一无二的——他们可以销售各种各样的产品，并且灵活地在正确的时间为他们锁定目标客户。他们使用基本的心理技巧——毕竟，销售是通过有效地增加购物者对产品或服务的需求来实现的。购物者通常有预先设想的需求，他们通过这些需求来判断产品的适用性。 「Q32」 售卖者试图找出这些要求是什么，然后用吸引个人购物者的语言来解释他们的产品。
G In his book Be a Great Salesperson, Richard Hession says that it is human nature to prefer to speak rather than to listen, and good sales people are able to use this to their advantage, encouraging shoppers to speak about their needs and offering to help them fill their needs. 「Q38」 This results in shoppers feeling that they are having a “consultation” rather than just being on the receiving end of a sales pitch 「Q39」 . Dr. Rob Yeung, a senior consultant at Kiddy and Partner, a business consulting company, says that salespeople will also build up rapport with a shopper by asking questions about family, lifestyle and hobbies, making them seem likeable while also obtaining information about the shopper's wants and needs.

理查德·赫申在他的书《成为一个伟大的销售人员》中说，比起倾听，更喜欢说话是人的本性，好的销售人员能够利用这一点，鼓励购物者说出他们的需求，并主动帮助他们满足他们的需求。 「Q38」 这让顾客觉得他们是在进行一次“咨询”，而不只是在推销辞令的接收端。 「Q39」 商业咨询公司Kiddy and Partner的高级顾问罗布·杨博士表示, 销售人员还会通过询问有关家庭、生活方式和爱好的问题，与购物者建立融洽的关系，让他看起来很受人喜欢，同时还能获得有关购物者需求的信息。
