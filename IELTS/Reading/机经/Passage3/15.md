Smell and Memory

嗅觉和记忆
A In recent years, the Proustian phenomenon in which specific odours can trigger or evoke specific memories has become a major talking point among scientists. For example, Rachel Herz, a cognitive neuroscientist at Brown University in Providence, Rhode Island, has discovered how sensory memories are shared across the brain, with different brain regions remembering the sights, smells, tastes and sounds of a particular experience. 「Q35」 "There is this unique connection between the sense of smell and the part of the brain that processes emotion," says Herz. Studies have shown that memories triggered by smells can be more emotional, as we have more detailed, than memories not related to smells. 「Q37」 When you inhale, odour molecules set brain cells dancing within a region known as the amygdala, apart of the brain that helps control emotion. Comparatively, other senses like taste or touch travel indirectly through other parts of the brain before they reach the amygdala. 「Q35」 The direct link between odours and the amygdale may help explain the emotional potency of smells.
近年来，普鲁斯特现象(特定的气味会引起特定的回忆)成为了科学家们的一个主要话题。例如，位于罗德岛州普罗维登斯市的布朗大学的认知神经学家雷切尔·赫兹发现了感觉记忆是如何在大脑中共享的，即不同的大脑区域分别记住特定经历中的景象、气味、味道和声音。赫兹说：“嗅觉和大脑处理情感的部分之间有着独特的联系。”研究表明，由气味触发的记忆比与气味无关的记忆更情绪化，也更详细。 「Q37」 当你吸气时，气味分子会让脑细胞在杏仁核区域舞动，杏仁核是大脑中帮助控制情绪的部分。相比之下，味觉或触觉等其他感觉在到达杏仁核之前，会通过大脑的其他部分间接传递。气味和杏仁核之间的直接联系可能有助于解释气味的情感力量。

B But the links don't stop there.The memory of smells has influence on other brain regions as well.In recent experiments, neuroscientists at University College London (UCL) asked 15 volunteers to view specific pictures at the same time they were exposed to unrelated odors. For instance, the subjects might see a photo of a duck paired with the scent of a rose, and then be asked to create a story linking the picture with the odour 「Q38」 Brain scans taken at the time revealed that the volunteers' brains were particularly active in a region known as the olfactory cortex, which is known to be involved in processing smells. A few minutes later, the volunteers were shown the same photo with no exposure to the rose smell. And in their brains, the olfactory cortex lit up again, even in the absence of the rose scent. 「Q28」 The fact that this happened gives evidence to the idea that people's sensory memory is distributed and retained by multiple regions of the brain. “Imagine going on a seaside holiday," says UCL team leader, Jay Gottfried."The sight of the waves becomes stored in one area, whereas the crash of the surf goes elsewhere, and the smell of seaweed in yet another place.There could be advantages of having memories spread around t 「Q34」 he brain.You can re-awake that memory from anyone of the sensory triggers."

但这些联系并不止于此。气味的记忆对大脑的其他区域也有影响。在最近的实验中，伦敦大学学院的神经科学家们让15名志愿者在观看特定的图片的同时接触一些无关的气味。例如，受试者可能会看到一张鸭子的照片并匹配玫瑰的气味， 然后要求他们创造一个故事，把照片和玫瑰气味联系起来。当时进行的脑部扫描显示，志愿者的大脑中一个名为嗅觉皮层的区城特别活跃，这个区城与处理气味有关。几分钟后，研究人员给志愿者看了同样的照片，但没有让他们接触玫瑰的气味。在他们的大脑中，即使在没有玫瑰香味的情况下，嗅觉皮层也再次被激活。这一事实证明，人的感觉记忆是由大脑的多个区域分配和保留的。伦敦大学学院团队负责人杰伊·戈特弗里德说：“想象一下去海边度假，海浪的景象存储在一个地方，而海浪的撞击声却存储在另一个地方，海草的气味又存储在另一个地方。让记忆在大脑中扩散可能是有好处的。 「Q34」 你可以从任何一种感觉触发物中重新唤醒那段记忆。”
C 「Q33」 Remembered smells may also carry extra emotional baggage, according to Herz. According to her research, memories evoked by odors are more emotional than memories evoked by other senses. In one recent study, Herz recruited 232 volunteers who had vivid memories associated with a particular perfume, such as Opium for women and Juniper Breeze from Bath and Body Works. She monitored the brainwaves of the volunteers as they sniffed that perfume and an unrelated perfume without knowing which was which.They were also shown photos of each perfume bottle. 「Q39」 「Q29」 The act of smelling the specified perfume maximizes the level of brain activity with the volunteers, in a region called the hippocampus, which aids in the forming of memories.
「Q33」 据赫兹说，记住的气味也会带来额外的情感负担。根据她的研究， 气味唤起的记忆比其他感官唤起的记忆更情绪化。在最近的一项研究中，赫兹招募了232名志愿者，他们对某种香水有着清晰的记忆，比如女性用的鸦片味香水， Bath & BodyWorks的杜松味香水。她在他们闻这种香水和另一种无关的香水时检测他们的脑电波，他们不知道这两种香水分别是哪一种。他们还看到了每个香水瓶的照 「Q39」 片。当志愿者闻到特定的香水时，他们大脑中一个叫作海马的区域的活动水平会达到最高，这个区域有助于记忆的形成。

D 「Q30」 Not only may odour-induced memories be more emotional, they are likely to be more detailed and vivid.Working with colleague John Downes, psychologist Simon Chu from the University of Liverpool started researching odour and memory partly because of his grandmother's stories about Chinese culture.He had noticed that as generations gathered to share oral histories, they would pass a small pot of spice or incense around； later, when they wanted to remember a story in as much detail as possible, they would pass the same smell around again. 「Q32」 “It kind of fits with a lot of anecdotal evidence on how smells can be really good reminders of past experiences, ”Chu says. The claim has been backed up by scientific research.In one experiment, Chu and Downes asked 42 volunteers to tell a life story, then tested to see whether odours such as coffee and cinnamon could help them remember more detail in the story. The outcome was satisfying.

「Q30」 气味引起的记忆不仅更情绪化，而且可能更详细、更生动。利物浦大学的心理学家西蒙·楚与同事约翰·唐斯合作，开始研究气味和记忆，部分原因是他祖母讲的关于中国文化的故事。他注意到，当几代人聚在一起分享口述历史时，他们会传递一小罐香料或熏香；之后，当他们想要尽可能详细地记住一个故事时，他们会再次传递同样的气味。 「Q32」 “这与很多关于气味如何能很好地提醒我们过去经历的轶事证据相吻合。”楚说。这种说法得到了科学研究的支持。在一项实验中，楚和唐斯让42名志愿者讲述一个生活故事，然后测试咖啡和肉桂等气味是否能帮助他们记住故事中的更多细节，结果令人满意。

E Despite such studies, not everyone is convinced that Proust can be scientifically analysed. 「Q36」 In the June issue of Chemical Senses, Chu and Downes exchanged critiques with renowned perfumer and chemist Joseph Stephan Jellinek.Jellinek chided the Liverpool researchers for, among other things, presenting the smells and asking the volunteers to think of memories, rather than seeing what memories were spontaneously evoked by the odours. Chu defends his position, saying that it is difficult to statistically measure people's memories 「Q40」 .Meanwhile, Jellinek has also been collecting anecdotal accounts of Proustian experiences, hoping to find out the sensory stimulus which elicits the experience. “I think there is a case to be made that surprise may be a major aspect of the Proust phenomenon, ” he says. “That's why people are so struck by these memories.” 「Q31」 We cannot be sure whether Marcel Proust ever experienced such a transcendental moment. Even so, his ideas regarding memory, written as fiction almost 100 years ago, continue to engage scientists today.

尽管有这些研究，并不是每个人都相信普鲁斯特可以被科学地分析。 「Q36」 在6月的《化学感官》杂志上，楚和唐斯与著名香水师和化学家约瑟夫·史蒂芬·杰利内克交换了意见。杰利内克斥责利物浦的研究人员，除了其他原因外，还因为他们展示了气味并要求志愿者去回忆，而不是观察气味会自发地唤起什么记忆。楚为自己的立场辩护说很难用数据来衡量人们的记忆。 「Q40」 与此同时，杰利内克也一直在收集普鲁斯特经历的轶事，希望找到引发这种经历的感官刺激。他说：“我认为有理由认为，‘意外’可能是普鲁斯特现象的一个主要方面。这就是为什么人们会被这些记忆所震撼。 「Q31」 ”我们不能确定马塞尔·普鲁斯特是否经历过这样一个超验的时刻。即便如此，他在近100年前以小说形式写出来的关于记忆的观点，仍然吸引着今天的科学家。
