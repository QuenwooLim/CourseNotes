The Power of Ants

蚂蚁的力量

A While farmers of Berne in Switzerland were taking pests to a court headed by an archbishop in 1476, Chinese farmers had a more practical approach to pest control. Rather than relying on divine intervention like their counterparts in Switzerland, the Chinese initially put their faith in frogs and ducks which were encouraged to snap up the pests in the paddies and check the occasional plague of locusts. But the notion of biological control began with an ant called yellow citrusant Oecophylla smaragdina. In the beginning, Chinese farmers randomly built ant nests here and there, but it did not take long before the growing demand led to the development of a thriving trade in nests and a new type of agriculture—ant farming.

当瑞士伯尔尼的农民在1476年将害虫告上由大主教领导的法庭时，中国农民有一个更实际的方法来控制害虫。中国人不像他们的瑞士同行那样依靠神的干预，他们最初相信青蛙和鸭子，鼓励它们捕捉稻田里的害虫，并阻止偶尔发生的蝗虫灾害。但是生物防治的概念是从一种叫黄柠檬猄蚁的蚂蚁开始的。起初，中国农民到处搭蚁巢，但不久需求的增长带动了蚁巢贸易的蓬勃发展和新型农业—蚂蚁养殖。

B China, as the ancestral home of oranges, lemons and other fruit trees, also faces the greatest diversity of citrus pests. The trees that produce the sweetest fruits usually attract a host of plant-eating insects. With so many hostile insects, fruit growers back then clearly had to find some way of protecting their orchards. Then the yellow citrus ant became remarkably popular as it could run fast and has a powerful nip, which would be perfect for killing a large number of insects that plagued the orange groves in southern China.

中国是柑橘、柠檬等果树的故乡，也是柑橘害虫种类最多的国家。那些结出最甜美果实的树通常会吸引大量以植物为食的昆虫。面对如此多的害虫，果农们显然得想办法保护他们的果园。后来，这种黄色的柑橘蚂蚁变得非常受欢迎，因为它跑得很快，而且有很强的咬力，这是杀死大量侵扰中国南方柑橘林的昆虫的完美工具。

C It was not until the early 20th century that the West discovered the Chinese orange growers'  secret weapon. At the time, Florida was suffering an epidemic of citrus canker. After trying all the methods that failed in the end, Walter Swingle, an American plant physiologist，was sent to China in search of varieties of orange plantations which were resistant to the disease. While Swingle was studying the citrus orchards around a city in Southern China, he accidentally came across the cultivated ant “grown” by villagers nearby who sold them to the orange growers.

直到20世纪初，西方才发现了中国橘子种植者的秘密武器。当时，佛罗里达正遭受柑橘溃疡病的流行。在尝试了所有办法以失败告终后，美国的植物生理学家沃特·斯温格尔被派往中国寻找各种能够抵抗这种疾病的柑橘种植园。当斯温格尔在中国南方一个城市研究柑橘果园时，他偶然发现了附近村民“种植”的栽培蚂蚁，这些蚂蚁被卖给了柑橘种植者。

D The earliest report of citrus ants at work among the orange trees appears in a book on tropical and subtropical botany in 304 AD, saying“The people of Chiao-Chih sell ants in bags of rush matting in their markets. The nests are like silk. The bags are all attached to twigs and leaves, with the ants kept inside the nests. The ants are reddish-yellow in colour, bigger than ordinary ants. In the south, if the orange trees do not have this kind of ant, the fruits will all be damaged by pests, and not a single fruit will survive. ”

最早关于柑橘类蚂蚁在橘子树林中活动的报道出现在公元304年一本关于热带和亚热带植物学的书中。书中写道：“chiao-chih人在他们的市场上用苇席袋来卖蚂蚁。蚁巢像丝绸一样。这些袋子都系在小树枝和树叶上，而蚂蚁则被关在巢穴里。蚂蚁是红黄色的，比普通蚂蚁大。在南方，如果橘子树没有这种蚂蚁，所有的果实都会被害虫破坏，没有一个果实能够存活。”

E“In the beginning, farmers depended on nests collected from the wild or bought at the market, where sales of nests were constant.  Also, people believe that orange trees that do not have ants may have wormy fruit. As a result, the people are eager to purchase nests for their orange trees, ”written by Liu Hsun in his book Strange Things Noted in the South in around AD 890. The industry quickly became more advanced. From the 10th century, people in the countryside began to trap ants in artificial nests baited with fat. In the year 1130, Chuang Chi-Yu noted that fruit-growing families purchased ants from sellers who made a business out of collecting and selling them.  He stated that they trapped ants by filling animal bladders with fat and placing them, with the cavities open, beside the ants' nests. They would then wait for the ants to travel into the bladders, and take the whole object away.  The practice was known as“rearing orange ants". These farmers would attach bladders to their trees, and over time, the ants would migrate to other trees and expand into new nests in large amounts.

“一开始，农民依靠从野外采集的或从市场上购买的蚁窝，市场的销售是稳定的。此外，人们相信没有蚂蚁的橘子树可能有虫蛀的果实。结果，人们急于为他们的橘子树买蚁窝，”约公元890年，刘间在他的《岭表录异》一书中这样写道。这个行业很快变得更加先进。从10世纪开始，乡下人开始用脂肪做诱饵把蚂蚁困在人工巢穴里。在1130年，庄智宇写道，种植水果的家庭从以收集和出售蚂蚁为业的商贩那里购买蚂蚁他说，他们把动物的膀胱填满脂肪，然后把它们放在蚁穴旁边，并把空腔打开，以此来诱捕蚂蚁。然后他们就会等着蚂蚁爬到膀胱里，然后把整个东西都拿走。这种做法被称为“饲养柑橘蚂蚁”。这些农民会在树上绑上膀胱，随着时间的推移，蚂蚁会迁移到其他树上，并大量地扩展到新的巢穴。

F Did it work? The orange growers clearly thought so.  One authority, Chai Ta-Chun, writing in 1700, stressed how important it was to keep the fruit trees free of insect pests, especially caterpillars.  He wrote, It is essential to eliminate them so that the trees are not injured. But hand labour is not nearly as efficient as ant power. ”Swingle was just as impressed. Yet despite these reports, many Western biologists were skeptical. In the West, the idea of using one insect to destroy another was new and highly controversial. The first breakthrough had come in 1888, when the infant orange industry in California had been saved from extinction by the Australian vedalia beetle. This beetle was the only thing that had made inroads into the explosion of cottony cushion scale that was likely to destroy the state's citrus crops. But，as Swingle now knew, California’s “first" was nothing of the sort. The Chinese had been an expert in biocontrol for many centuries.

这样有用吗?橘子种植者显然是这么认为的。权威人士柴大春在1700年的著作中强调，保持果树不受虫害(尤其是毛毛虫)是多么重要。他写道：“必须清除它们，这样树木才不会受到伤害。但是，手工劳动的效率远不如蚂蚁的力量。" 斯温格尔非常钦佩。然而，尽管有这些报告，许多西方生物学家还是持怀疑态度。在西方，用一种昆虫来消灭另一种昆虫的想法是新鲜的，而且极具争议性。第一次突破是在1888年，当时澳大利亚的瓢虫拯救了加州刚刚起步的橘子产业，使其免于灭绝。这种甲虫是唯一一种对吹绵蚧的爆发有影响的东西，吹绵蚧有可能摧毁这个州的柑橘类作物。但是，正如斯温格尔现在所知道的，加州的“第一”完全不是那样的。几个世纪以来，中国人一直是生物防治方面的专家。

G There was one known drawback of using ants—citrus ants do nothing to control mealy bugs, a waxy-coated scale insect which can do considerable damage to fruit trees, and for this reason Western scientists were doubtful about the practice. In fact, the reverse is true—the ants protect the pests in order to eat the sweet honeydew they secrete. The orange growers denied that this was an issue. In the 1980s, research showed that the growers were right. When the pest thrives under the protection of the ants, they are often targeted by parasites，limiting the harm they can cause. Orange growers who rely on carnivorous ants rather than poisonous chemicals maintain a better balance of species in their orchards. Ants are best at controlling larger insect pests, and there are also other predatory species which control the numbers of scale insects and aphids. Ultimately, ants are more effective and do less damage than chemical extermination.

使用蚂蚁有一个众所周知的缺点—柑橘类蚂蚁对粉蚧毫无办法，粉蚧是一种被蜡包裹的蚧虫，会对果树造成相当大的损害。因此西方科学家对这种做法持怀疑态度。事实上，反过来也是正确的蚂蚁为了吃它们分泌的甜蜜露而保护这种害虫。橘子种植者否认这是一个问题。然而，在20世纪80年代，研究表明种植者是对的。当这种害虫在蚂蚁的保护下茁壮成长时，它们经常成为寄生虫的攻击目标，从而限制了它们可能造成的危害。依靠食肉蚂蚁而不是有毒化学物质的橘子种植者在他们的果园里保持了更好的物种平衡。蚂蚁最擅长控制较大的害虫，也有其他食肉物种控制介壳虫和蚜虫的数量。最终，蚂蚁比化学根除更有效，造成的伤害更小。
