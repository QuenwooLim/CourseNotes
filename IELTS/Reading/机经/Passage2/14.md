A In September 1996, a competition was organized by the Financial Times in association with the London Borough of Southwark to design a new footbridge across the Thames, which at that time would be London's only pedestrian bridge and the first new crossing on this part of the Thames. The news quickly spread nationwide and worldwide through TV stations and radio network. The competition attracted over 200 entries 「Q20」 . In the end, architect Norman Foster, sculptor Sir Anthony Caro as well as Arup (an engineer team) took the top prize. The Millennium Bridge represents a creative collaboration between architecture, art and engineering. As a key element in London's pedestrian infrastructure, this bridge has created new routes for Londoners and encouraged new life on the embankment alongside St Paul's.

1996年9月，英国《金融时报》与伦敦南华克区联合举办了一场设计横跨泰晤士河的新人行桥的竞赛，这在当时将是伦敦唯一的一座人行桥，也是泰晤士河此段的首个新跨越点。消息通过电视台和广播网迅速传遍全国和全世界。比赛吸引了200多名参赛者。最终，建筑师诺曼·福斯特爵士、雕塑家安东尼·卡罗爵士以及工程师团队奥雅纳获得了最高奖项。这座千禧桥代表了建筑、艺术和工程之间的创造性合作。作为伦敦行人基础设施的一个关键元素，这座桥为伦敦人创造了新的道路，并鼓励了圣保罗旁边的堤岸上的新生活。

Normal human activities like jumping, running, swaying and even walking can create horizonal forces which cause excessive dynamic vibration in a lateral direction on the bridge. As they felt the bridge moving, the pedestrians would naturally have adjusted their walking style to the same rhythm as the bridge. These altered footsteps would have made the motion worse—something like when four people in a boat all stand up together. As more pedestrians copied the movements of the bridge, the dramatic swaying increased, as seen in the video.
