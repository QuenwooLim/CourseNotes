Earth Formation and the Origin of Species

地球形成和物种起源

A “Why do some species die and some live? The answer was clear：on the whole the best fitted lived.  From the effects of disease, the healthiest escaped； from enemies, the strongest, the swiftest or the most cunning； from famine, the best hunter. . . 「Q21」 This self-acting process would improve the race, because in every generation the inferior would inevitably be killed off and the superior would remain, that is, the fittest would survive. ” It is a reflection written by Alfred Russel Wallace in 1858 that framed a theory of the evolutionary origin of species.

“为什么有些物种灭绝，有些物种会活下来?" 答案很明显，大体上说，最合适的都活下来了。最健康的躲过了疾病；最强大最敏捷或最狡猾的躲过了天敌；最好的捕猎者在饥荒中活了下来……这种自发的过程会改善种族，因为在每一代生物中，劣等的必然会被杀死，而优等的会留下来，也就是说，适者生存。”阿尔弗雷德·罗素·华莱士在1858年写的一篇反思中提出了物种进化起源的理论。

And Wallace attributed to his years of travel in the Amazon Basin and in the Indonesia-Australian Archipelago in the 1850s.

F 「Q18」 After reading Geographical Distribution of Animals by Wallace, Alfred Wegener, a German polar researcher and geophysicist, cited the three clear divisions of Australian animals to support his own theory of continental displacement. Interestingly, Wallace had discovered that animal species that had established themselves in southwestern Australia had a resemblance in structure to animals in South Africa, Madagascar, but had no affinity with animal species in Asia. Wallace was also able to document that Australian marsupials and monotremes were clearly linked to species in South America, the Moluccas, and various Pacific islands, and, curiously, no links were found to species in nearb 「Q25」 y Indonesia. From this and related data, Wegener concluded that the broadly accepted “land bridge” theory could not account for this distribution of animals and that only his theory of “continental drift” could explain it.

「Q18」 德国极地研究者、地球物理学家阿尔弗雷德·韦格纳在阅读了华莱士的《动物的地理分布》之后，引用了澳大利亚动物的三种明显的分类来支持他自己的大陆迁移理论。有趣的是，华莱士之前发现生活在澳大利亚西南部的动物在结构上与南非、马达加斯加的动物相似，但与亚洲的动物没有亲缘关系。华莱士还发现，澳大利亚的有袋动物和单孔目动物与南美洲、摩鹿加群岛和太平洋诸岛的物种有明显的联系，奇怪的是，它们与附近印度尼西亚的物种没有联系。 「Q25」 从这些现象和相关资料中，韦格纳得出结论：人们广泛接受的“陆桥”理论不能解释这种动物的分布，只有他的大陆漂移理论才能解释。

「Q26」 韦格纳摒弃的一个理论，认为植物和动物曾经在迁徙中穿过现在已被淹没的洲际陆地连接。 「Q15」 1885年，一位欧洲顶尖地质学家爱德华·苏斯创立了一个理论，认为随着坚硬的地球冷却下来，它的上地壳会收缩、起皱，就像衰老的苹果的果皮一样。苏斯认为，地球上的海洋现在填补了曾经相邻的高原之间的褶皱。

H 「Q27」 Although many zoogeographers no longer consider the Wallace Line to be a regional boundary, it does represent an abrupt limit of distribution for many major animal groups.  Various fish, bird, and mammal groups which are abundant on one side of the Wallace Line are either absent or poorly represented on the other side. 「Q19」 Today, we know that we live on a dynamic Earth with shifting, colliding and separating tectonic plates, not a "withering skin", and the main debate in the field of biogeography has changed to centre around "dispersalism" versus "vicarianism", or in other terms, unrestricted radiation of species on the one hand and the development of barriers to migration on the other. Dispersion usually will see a proportion of the ancestral population actively move across a geographic barrier to form a similar species which later transformed into a new species. However, the process of vicariance is more passive as the original species was divided reluctantly by a geographical barrier. Years later, both species may go through a series of evolution and results in new species.

「Q27」 虽然许多动物地理学家不再认为华莱士线是一个区域界线，但它确实代表了许多主要动物种群的分布的骤然界限。在华莱士线的一边大量存在的各种各样的鱼类、鸟类和哺乳动物群体，在线的另一边要么是不存在的，要么是很少出现的。 「Q19」 今天，我们知道我们生活在一个有着不断漂移、碰撞和分离的地壳板块的动态地球上，而不是一张“枯萎的皮肤”上，生物地理学领域的主要辩论也改为围绕着“分散主义”与“隔离主义”，或者换句话说，一方面是物种的无限制放射，另一方面是迁移壁垒的发展。分散通常是说一部分祖先种群积极地跨越地理障碍，形成一个类似的物种，然后转变成一个新的物种。然而，地理隔离的过程是比较被动的，因为原始物种是被地理屏障勉强分割的。几年后，这两个物种可能会经历一系列的进化，形成新的物种。
